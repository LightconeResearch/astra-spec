- **Date:** 2026-11-04
- **Branch:** `v0.1-schema`, at `a0465b7`

- **Status:** every decision taken by Tom on 2026-11-04 (the entries and the decisions table carry them)
- **Method:** every finding's cause, fix and route were extracted; two independent groupings (by cause and by the edit that resolves them) were reconciled into fixes; each fix was written against its findings and the sources, then reviewed adversarially (does it resolve each finding it claims, is its route right, does it hide a decision) and revised where the review asked; findings left unresolved were swept into further fixes.

**Decisions already made** (Tom, 2026-11-04), which the plan applies:

1. `astra.yaml` needn't be Windows-compatible. Paths are written as git writes them in a commit (tidy-up change 27), and a tool on Windows maps them, as git does.
2. Paths are case-sensitive, which protects everyone: a path names the file git holds at exactly that path, case (and Unicode form) included, on every machine.
3. Git's limits apply: no ASTRA rule bounds the length of ids, formats or paths.

The tidy-up RFC is cited by the change numbers of its current draft, `NNNN-settling-the-schema-for-v0.1.md` (29 changes); the findings cite the earlier draft's numbers.

## Summary

The 163 findings are grouped into 59 fixes, and astra-tools' design adds a 60th (X60): 32 just do, 18 tidy-up RFC, 2 when RFC and 8 checker and runner phase. 35 of them needed a decision, and Tom took every one on 2026-11-04; his answers moved X46 to just do and dropped `PIN_LIKE_COMMENT` (X49).

The big themes are:
- **Spec text has drifted from RFCs 0004, 0005 and 0007.** This covers driving, runner rules, records, bytes and stand-ins.
- **The test harness and tooling don't check everything they claim to.** This covers the invalid-fixture test, the generator, the error map, the §9 extractor and the YAML loader.
- **The parse layer and the reading of git are underspecified.** This covers YAML 1.2, JSON forms, paths, symlinks and submodules.
- **Some check-phase questions are left open.** These are planned results, staleness, bytes and versions.

Two findings are only partly resolved: F061, by X16 and X47 together, and F140, whose .inf and .nan cases go to X31.

Every just do fix can now start except X04, which waits for X33 and X35. The 8 checker and runner phase fixes need only short notes in design §12 and §13 now, so they can be put off without risk.

## For astra-tools

What the decided plan means for astra-tools' v0.1 design
(`astra-tools/design/2026-11-04-v0.1-tooling-design.md`, branch `v0.1-design`).
The tidy-up items below are decided but not yet in the specification: they land
with their changes to the tidy-up RFC.

**The rulings its §14 asks for:**

- **One result reached under two names** (X52, landing now): a result's name is
  its output's id after the driven declarations that lead to it, through the
  fewest of them, then the first in byte order. That name gives the record's
  place and its `address`.
- **An analysis with no decisions reading a per-universe output** (X34, moved
  from X51): an analysis with no decisions and no universes of its own counts
  as driven by every universe of its driver.
- **Agreeing spellings of one decision:** no warning in v0.1. The point stays
  open in design §13 (X04).
- **Which `spec_version`s a validator accepts** (X38, X59): those it has the
  schema for, which is 0.1.0 for now. A file with a missing or malformed
  `spec_version` is validated against the schema of the validator's own
  version. From 0.1.0 on, a checker keeps the record schema of every release.
- **Quoting of substituted paths:** adopted as X60. Placeholders are
  substituted verbatim, and authors quote.
- **Evidence ids:** stay removed.

**Its other points:**

- **A tag to pin against:** a 0.1.0 pre-release once the contract fixes land
  (Order of work, steps 1 to 4). Files keep `spec_version: "0.1.0"`, and X30
  says what a pre-release's `$id` carries.
- **`conformance/` in astra-spec from the start:** agreed. It stays out of the
  wheel (D7). Its `spec/` cases are normative; its `tools/` shapes (the plan,
  the check report's details, the view) are agreements between the tools,
  marked non-normative.
- **Analysis-159's second code:** X14 removes it, so conformance comparisons
  needn't allow for it.
- **Parametric outputs and multi-file inputs:** recorded for later in design
  §12 (X28).

**What changes in its design:**

- **`PIN_LIKE_COMMENT` is dropped** (X49). Its case goes, and no loader needs
  YAML comments.
- **Stand-ins** (X46): the specification says nothing about which exist. The
  `MISSING_INPUT_FILE` and `ABSENT_RESULT` cases with LFS, annex or DVC files
  move from `spec/` to `tools/`.
- **Loading `astra.yaml`:** a non-string key is `NON_STRING_KEY`, judged on the
  YAML node before conversion to JSON (X31); one byte pre-scan rejects raw
  control characters and tabs (X32); only core tags, no anchors or aliases, and
  at most one document (X36).
- **Records** are strict JSON, with no BOM and no repeated name. A broken
  record leaves its result `missing`, reported as `INVALID_RECORD` with the
  underlying code as its reason (X37).
- **No cascades** (X38): a rule whose subject another broken rule leaves
  undetermined isn't checked or reported. That matches the validation report's
  `unchecked[]`.
- **Driving** (X34): judged on the effective selection, per chain of
  declarations; a reference's last declaration decides; a pin resolves in the
  frame of the file it is written in. The root is never driven (X06).
- **Paths** (X35): case- and Unicode-exact against git's tree, on every
  machine. The specification has no Windows rule.
- **Runners** (X02): a short list of MUSTs that protect what a record says;
  `UNDECLARED_READ` becomes a SHOULD on recipes, which a runner may enforce.
- **Location selectors** (X39): an optional `conformsTo`; without it, the value
  is a PDF fragment (RFC 8118). The view's parsed fragment values follow
  `conformsTo`.

### X05. §10 'From an error to its code': each validator described on its own, and only errors that make the instance fail are mapped

- **Resolves:** F049, F105, F106, F115, F120 · **Size:** M · **Depends on:** X14 · **Decision:** no

**The problem.** §10's "From an error to its code" describes `jsonschema` and Ajv as one validator that reports "the innermost errors". Neither does that exactly (F105). Tools that use standard output get two kinds of innermost error that don't make the instance fail: an untaken `if`'s own failure, and a failing branch of a `oneOf` that passed. The first has no entry and the second gets the wrong code (F049). The jsonschema bullet blames the omitted `if` for the schema path not being the location, but only the unnamed `$ref` causes that (F106). The paragraph also doesn't say where a bad map key is (F115), and it leaves out Ajv's `uniqueItems` departures (F120).

**The fix.**
- Spec §10, opening paragraph, rewritten in this sense: a tool maps each error that makes the instance fail. That means the failing assertions, plus a `oneOf`, `not`, `if` or `propertyNames` that fails as a whole, together with the errors that say why a failing `oneOf` failed. Each has an entry. A tool drops any error inside an `if` subschema (JSON Schema 2019-09 core §9.2.2.1: its outcome "has no direct effect"). It also drops any error inside a `oneOf`/`anyOf` branch unless that combinator is reported as failing, and the applicators that standard output lists above an error. Any validator then gives the same set of (instance location, code) pairs, though the number of errors may differ.
- The jsonschema bullet says that it reports the errors inside a failing `then` and inside `propertyNames`, never the `if` or `propertyNames` itself, and that a failing `oneOf` comes as one error with its branch errors in `context`. The parenthetical about `if` goes. For a bad key, `error.instance` is the key.
- The Ajv bullet says that Ajv reports both levels (the inner error and the `if`/`propertyNames`/`oneOf` above it, with the same code), and that the key is `propertyName`, or `params.propertyName` on the `propertyNames` error. It also says that Ajv's `uniqueItems` skips items of the wrong type and misses a repeated `__proto__`. An Ajv-based tool checks for `__proto__` itself, and a comparison of code sets ignores `DUPLICATE_ENTRY` beside a `WRONG_TYPE`.
- One sentence before the bullets: a bad key is reported at the map's pointer, since a JSON Pointer can't name a key, and a tool reports the key with the code.
- `tests/conftest.py` `absolute_keyword_location` docstring: the `if` clause is dropped. The comment at `tools/rules.py:410` and the `REPORTING` docstring in `tests/test_rules.py` cite §10.
- `tests/test_rules.py`: jschon goes into the dev group, pinned. A new test applies §10's filter to jschon's basic output for every schema-layer invalid fixture. It asserts that every remaining error has an entry and that the (pointer, code) set equals jsonschema's. Analysis-150 and Analysis-166 are the two fixtures that show the leak.
- Design §12 records the Ajv `__proto__` bug. The upstream Ajv issue is filed if Tom agrees.
- The `dependentRequired` option is not taken. X14 reshapes these `if`/`then` pairs (typed presence, an `if: {type: object}` guard), and §10's filter covers any `if` it adds.

**Why this route.** §10 only explains how to use D13's error map. The fix restores what `rules.json` already means and adds nothing to it: no change to what is valid, and no change to any code. The tidy-up draft doesn't mention `rules.json`.

### X30. Each JSON Schema's `$id` names its version

- **Resolves:** F151 · **Size:** M · **Decision:** decided

**The problem.** Each JSON Schema's `$id` is `https://w3id.org/astra/` and its file name (`justfile:35`). A release changes only `$comment`, so every version of a schema has the same `$id`, and so does every one of the 266 `rules.json` error keys (F151). A checker still holds several versions at once: `INVALID_RECORD` uses "the record schema of its `spec_version`", and X59 has a checker keep every release's record schema from 0.1.0 on. JSON Schema 2019-09 §8.2.4.5 allows one schema per URI. Ajv throws on the second version, and Python's `referencing` silently keeps one. §10 doesn't say which version a URI names. Between releases, `_stage-versioned` (`justfile`, the recipe after `_pytest`) stamps the latest tag, so main declares `0.1.0` for content that isn't 0.1.0, and a clone without tags stamps `0.0.0` and fails `just test` (design §12). And astra-tools needs a 0.1.0 pre-release to pin against, which `just release` can't make: it accepts only `X.Y.Z`.

**The fix.**
- `tools/jsonschema_generator.py`: `--id` takes a `{version}` placeholder. The schema's version fills it, the same value that fills `$comment`.
- `justfile`, `gen-schema`: the three `--id` arguments become `{{schema_namespace}}{version}/<file>`. Update the comment on `schema_namespace` and the one above `gen-schema`. `tools/rules.py` builds its keys from `$id` (line 369), so it needs no change.
- `justfile`, `_stage-versioned`: the version is `$RELEASE_VERSION` when set; otherwise the tag at HEAD (`git describe --tags --exact-match --match 'v[0-9]*'`) without its `v`; otherwise `dev`. It fails on a value that is none of `X.Y.Z`, `X.Y.Z` followed by `aN`, `bN` or `rcN`, or `dev`. A clone without tags then stamps `dev`, which is what main holds, so the design §12 tagless-clone item goes.
- `justfile`, `release`: the version may be a pre-release, `^[0-9]+\.[0-9]+\.[0-9]+((a|b|rc)[0-9]+)?$`. This is PEP 440's normal form, and it is what uv-dynamic-versioning (`style = "pep440"`) makes of the tag `v0.1.0rc1`, so the tag, the wheel's version and the `$id` segment are one string (`0.1.0rc1`). `0.1.0-rc.1` is rejected: PEP 440 would normalise the wheel to `0.1.0rc1`, and the ids would differ from it. After the tag, the recipe runs `RELEASE_VERSION=dev just gen-schema` and commits "Back to dev after vX.Y.Z", so only the tagged commit holds the version. HEAD is then that commit, and the "next steps" push (`git push --atomic origin HEAD vX.Y.Z`) stays as it is.
- What a pre-release carries. Its files carry the pre-release's full version, in `$id` (`https://w3id.org/astra/0.1.0rc1/analysis.schema.json`) and in each `$comment`, `rules.json`'s included. A pre-release is a candidate for 0.1.0, not 0.1.0, so its ids and keys differ from the release's. The documents it checks still declare `spec_version: "0.1.0"`: the `spec_version` pattern has no pre-release form, and none is added. A pre-release isn't a release, so X59's duty to keep earlier releases' record schemas doesn't cover it. A record written while 0.1.0rc1 was current declares 0.1.0 and is read with 0.1.0's schema.
- `.github/workflows/deploy-docs-on-tag.yaml`: a pre-release tag deploys the site at its own version (`/0.1.0rc1/`) and doesn't move `latest`. The alias is `latest` only when the version is `X.Y.Z`, and `docs-deploy` takes an empty alias to mean none. A GitHub release marked as a pre-release still publishes to PyPI, where pip skips `0.1.0rc1` unless asked. astra-tools' `astra-spec ~= 0.1.0` excludes it, so astra-tools pins `astra-spec == 0.1.0rc1` until 0.1.0 is out.
- Spec §10: the lead-in sentence ("each is published at `https://w3id.org/astra/` followed by its file name") gains the version segment, and so do the table and the three keys of the `rules.json` excerpt. The `$id` paragraph says, in gist: "Each JSON Schema's `$id` is its URI in its own version, `https://w3id.org/astra/<version>/<file>`, so versions are distinct resources and every `rules.json` key names its version. A pre-release's version is `X.Y.Z` followed by `aN`, `bN` or `rcN`; it is a candidate for `X.Y.Z`, and the documents it checks declare `X.Y.Z`. `https://w3id.org/astra/<file>` redirects to the latest release and is no schema's `$id`."
- Tests: `test_contract.py` (lines 29, 300-302, 311-313) checks that `$comment` ends in `X.Y.Z`, a pre-release or `dev`, and that `$id` is the namespace, that version, `/`, then the file name. `test_rules.py` (lines 33-42) builds its prefixes from the committed `$comment` the same way. `test_generator.py` gets a test of `{version}` in `--id`, with a release, a pre-release and `dev`.
- Design §3 (the table, the `$id` bullet, and the example key on line 113), the redirect item in §12 (the redirects serve `<version>/<file>`, pre-releases included; `dev` is served nowhere), and in CLAUDE.md the release flow (pre-release versions, the `dev` commit, `latest` moving only on a release), the stamping sentence ("the tag at HEAD, or `dev`"; the tags are needed only at a tagged commit) and the "pin to" sentence.

**Decided.** Option (A), as recommended: versioned ids in the `X.Y.Z` form, `dev` unless HEAD is at a tag, and a `dev` regeneration commit after each release's tag. A pre-release's ids carry its full PEP 440 version (`0.1.0rc1`), while the files it checks keep `spec_version: "0.1.0"`. Not taken: unversioned ids that name the latest (B), `X.Y` ids, and stamping the latest tag between releases.

**Why this route.** No accepted RFC or tidy-up change names a schema URI, and design §11 keeps the generated JSON Schemas out of the RFC: they are "a published artefact of the implementation". Pre-releases and the release recipe are tooling. Nothing changes what is valid: the `spec_version` pattern is untouched.

**Done when.** `just check-generated` passes on a commit with no tag, with every file stamped `dev`. The contract test asserts that `$id` matches `$comment`. ### X31. Change 29: values with no JSON form fail at parse, and integers stay in the safe range

- **Resolves:** F001, F121, F163 · **Size:** M · **Depends on:** X09, X10, X37 · **Decision:** decided

**The problem.** Change 29 says a file "means the same as the JSON it converts to", but some YAML values have no JSON form, and nothing says what they become. A `true:`, `1:` or `~:` key is WRONG_TYPE in Python, VALID or INVALID_ID in Ajv, and INVALID_ID by §4 and §9 (F001). `.inf`, `.nan` and numbers beyond double range get different codes and sometimes opposite verdicts, in `astra.yaml` and in records (`"size": -1e400`, a 400-digit `size`) (F121). A `\uD800` escape gives a valid path that Python and Node resolve to different files (F163).

**The fix.**
- Tidy-up change 29, a new paragraph, "Every document has one JSON form". It covers `astra.yaml` and records. Every map key is a string: a key that isn't one is `NON_STRING_KEY`, a new parse code, whatever else is wrong with it (`.nan:` and `1e400:` included). Every number is finite and doesn't overflow a double: `.inf`, `-.inf`, `.nan` and `1e400` are INVALID_SYNTAX. Every string is a sequence of Unicode scalar values: a lone surrogate is INVALID_SYNTAX. A tool checks all of this on the parsed nodes, before converting them. Migration step: "quote a key that YAML reads as a boolean, number or null." The compatibility line reads "Changes 22, 28 and 29 change RFC-0005's records". Records get the rule through X37's change 30 and its `load_json` hooks (`parse_float`, `parse_int`, the surrogate walk).
- Spec §3: state the same rules in "Every document". The duplicate-key bullet adds "only string keys are compared". The commit-id warning, written together with X19, says that an unquoted id made of digits and one `e` is a float, and that it fails at parse when its exponent overflows (`1234e` followed by 35 digits). Today that id is WRONG_TYPE. Either way, the fix is to quote it.
- §9: a `NON_STRING_KEY` row: "Every map key is a string; a key that isn't is this code whatever else is wrong with it." `DUPLICATE_KEY` adds "comparing string keys only". `INVALID_SYNTAX` gets the shared wording from X37. `INVALID_VALUE` adds "every integer is at most 2^53−1".
- `maximum_value: 9007199254740991` on `Evidence.version`, `ProvenanceRecord.size`, `RecordedInput.size` and `Resources.gpus`. `tools/rules.py` gives `maximum` the INVALID_VALUE default, and §10 and CLAUDE.md say so.
- `tools/jsonschema_generator.py:61`: `propertyNames` holds only `pattern`. The nine `propertyNames/type` entries leave `rules.json`, which replaces that part of X01. X01's `1:` fixture becomes a NON_STRING_KEY parse fixture.
- §10, for JS tools: walk `parseDocument`'s keys before `toJS()`. Either pass `uniqueKeys: (a, b) => both are string scalars && a.value === b.value`, or report NON_STRING_KEY before any DUPLICATE_KEY in `doc.errors`. Reject a number that isn't `Number.isFinite`, and a string that isn't `isWellFormed()`. Keep Ajv's `strictNumbers` on.
- `tests/conftest.py`: `construct_mapping` raises `NonStringKeyError` for a key node not tagged `!!str`. The check runs after X10's composer override, which makes a `!`-tagged key a string, and before X10's (tag, canonical form) duplicate check. X09's `parse_code` maps the error to NON_STRING_KEY, and `PARSE_CODES` includes it. `construct_core_float` and `construct_core_int` raise when the value overflows a double or isn't finite. A post-load walk rejects U+D800–U+DFFF. `tests/test_loader.py` gets one case for each.
- Parse fixtures: `true:`, `1:`, `~:`, `? [a, b]`, a `"true"`/`true` pair, a `1:`/`1.0:` pair, a `.nan:` key, `version: .inf`, `version: 1e400`, `path: "data/\uD800.csv"`, and a record with `"path": "data/\ud800.csv"`. Schema fixtures: `gpus: 9007199254740992`, and a record whose `size` is that number. The record fixture with `"size": 1e400` is X37's. Add any fixture the linters reject to their exclude lists. Update CLAUDE.md Tests and design §9.

**Decision** (Tom, 2026-11-04: decided as recommended).
(1) The code for a value with no JSON form.
- (a) Recommended: NON_STRING_KEY for keys, INVALID_SYNTAX for numbers and surrogates. Keys get their own code because authors hit them in ordinary files: a boolean decision's `true`/`false` options, or numbered options. Their one remedy is to quote the key. The other values are ones no file means to hold. They are the YAML forms of what RFC 8259 already rules out or leaves undefined (`NaN`, `Infinity`, an unpaired surrogate), which a record's parser reports as INVALID_SYNTAX under X37. A JSON record can't hold a non-string key, so NON_STRING_KEY arises only in YAML.
- (b) One new code, `NO_JSON_FORM`, for all three. This is uniform, but `true:` no longer says "quote it", and a record's `NaN` and an `astra.yaml`'s `.nan` get different codes.
- (c) INVALID_SYNTAX for all three. This adds no new code, but the commonest case gets the vaguest code.
- (d) The findings' own codes, raised on the node: INVALID_ID for a key (F001), WRONG_TYPE for a number (F121). These are schema-layer codes raised before conversion, so no keyword in the error map gives them and no pointer is reported. INVALID_ID also misnames `1:` among an entry's fields, where no id is expected, and WRONG_TYPE misnames `cpus: .inf`.
- (e) Stringify keys and check them as ids. Every language then needs a canonical-form table (`~` is `""` in `yaml` and `"null"` in Python), and a `"true"`/`true` pair collides.

(2) Numbers. (a) Recommended: overflow and non-finite values fail at parse, and integers take the maximum, so both languages read every integer exactly. The consequence is the commit-id case above. (b) Use F121's own fix, WRONG_TYPE. This has the problem of (1d). (c) Leave numbers to each language. Verdicts then still differ beyond 2^1024.

(3) Surrogates. (a) Recommended: reject them at parse. A lone surrogate can't be encoded in UTF-8 in any string. (b) Use F163's alternative: exclude U+D800–U+DFFF from the two path classes (INVALID_PATH). Labels and descriptions then keep lone surrogates, and the RDF and UTF-8 output can't carry them.

**Why this route.** Each choice changes what is valid. `true:` is valid in Ajv today, and a size of 2^60 is valid in both languages. Change 29 says only that "the core schema's types are JSON's" and doesn't say what a value with no JSON form becomes. No change in the draft covers these cases.

**Done when.** Every new fixture passes under X09's exact-code check, in Python and in a JS check following §10. No `propertyNames` in `generated/` holds a `type`. `just test` is green. Do this in one pass with X36 and X32, which edit the same §3 bullets and §9 `INVALID_SYNTAX` row. Land it together with X37, which cites this paragraph.

### X32. Change 29: the bytes and characters of an astra.yaml, checked by one pre-scan

- **Resolves:** F066, F067, F141, F156, F158 · **Size:** M · **Decision:** decided

**The problem.** ASTRA has no rule of its own about the bytes and characters of an `astra.yaml`. It leaves them to "any strict YAML 1.2 loader" (§2, §3, §9 `INVALID_SYNTAX`), and both reference loaders depart from YAML 1.2 here. ruamel reads NEL, LS and PS as line breaks, rejects DEL and C1 even inside quotes, rejects tab separation and detects no UTF-32 (F066, F067, F156). The `yaml` package checks no printable set, keeps a lone CR as content and takes only a string, so its caller picks the decoding (F141, F156, F158). The same bytes get different verdicts or values in Python and TypeScript.

**The fix.**
- Tidy-up change 29, a paragraph and migration steps. An `astra.yaml` is UTF-8, with a byte order mark allowed only as its first character. It holds no tab, no control character but LF and the CR of a CRLF (so no DEL, C1 or NEL), and no LS, PS, U+FEFF, U+FFFE or U+FFFF. A line ends in LF or CRLF. Such characters are written as escapes in double quotes (`\t`, `\x92`, `\N`, `\L`, `\P`, `﻿`). The rule is `astra.yaml`'s only: a record's text is RFC 8259's, which allows a raw tab between tokens and raw DEL, C1, LS and PS in strings (X37 states its bytes). Migration: re-save UTF-16 and Latin-1 files as UTF-8, replace tabs, convert lone-CR line endings, escape raw controls.
- Spec §3: the rule as a new first `astra.yaml` bullet under `INVALID_SYNTAX`, saying it doesn't apply to records and that a validator decodes the bytes strictly and checks the characters before loading. The records sentence "by the same parse and schema rules" gets "(the character rule aside)", unless X37 rewrites it. §2's parse row reads "one file's bytes". §10: each loader's departures, and the pre-scan (`TextDecoder('utf-8', {fatal: true})` before `yaml`).
- §9 `INVALID_SYNTAX` Rule cell, the change 29 paragraph and the new §3 bullets are edited in one pass with X31 and X36. The cell, in that order: "Every `astra.yaml` is UTF-8 holding only the characters §3 allows, each line ending in LF or CRLF; it is a well-formed YAML 1.2 stream of at most one document, with no `%YAML` directive other than 1.2, no anchor or alias and no tag but the core schema's; in every document each string is a sequence of Unicode scalar values and each number is finite and within double range; and every record is RFC 8259 JSON text in UTF-8 with no byte order mark." Regenerate `rules.json`.
- `tests/conftest.py`: `load_yaml` reads bytes. `load_yaml` and `load_yaml_text` run one pre-scan: a strict `utf-8-sig` decode, then `[\x00-\x09\x0B\x0C\x0E-\x1F\x7F-\x9F  ﻿￾￿]|\r(?!\n)`, raising a ruamel `YAMLError`. `fixture_header` reads the first two LF-ended lines as ASCII bytes. The docstrings and `test_loader.py:1` say YAML 1.2 plus the pre-scan. This supersedes X10's "a tab only where YAML allows one" bullet, dead code once the pre-scan rejects every tab; X10 drops it.
- `tests/test_loader.py`: each forbidden character in plain and quoted scalars, the escape for each, CRLF, a leading BOM, U+FEFF in a plain scalar and at a line start, and UTF-16 and UTF-32 as byte cases.
- Parse fixtures (UTF-8 or Latin-1, ASCII headers): a tab, a raw C1 in quotes, a raw LS, a lone CR, Latin-1, an inner U+FEFF. Each goes in `check-yaml`'s exclude and `.yamllint.yaml`'s ignore; the tab, CR and U+FEFF ones in `trailing-whitespace`'s and `end-of-file-fixer`'s excludes; all in a new `.gitattributes` as `-text`. CLAUDE.md's Tests paragraph names these hand-kept lists and the pre-scan.

**Decision** (Tom, 2026-11-04: decided as recommended). (1) Encoding. UTF-8 only, recommended: every validator decodes the same way. Or YAML §5.2's UTF-8/16/32, which every validator must then detect. (2) Characters. Forbid them as above, recommended: one context-free pre-scan. Or YAML 1.2's rules (any non-C0 character in quotes, NEL/LS/PS as content, a lone CR as a break), which needs a context-aware scanner in each stack. U+FEFF past the first character: forbid it, recommended, since YAML 1.2.2 [27] excludes it from plain scalars and comments while ruamel and `yaml` 2.9.1 both keep it as content; or state that it is allowed. (3) Tabs. Forbid raw tabs, recommended: YAML already bars them in indentation, no tracked file holds one, and the pre-scan stays context-free. The cost: a block scalar can't hold a tab, so a TSV, Makefile or code snippet in a `description` becomes a double-quoted scalar written with `\t`. Or keep tab separation: extend X10's scanner patch to accept it, add valid tab fixtures, and write "after white space" in §4's `PIN_LIKE_COMMENT`.

**Why this route.** Change 29 says only "YAML 1.2, read under its core schema". Each rule here narrows YAML 1.2.2 §5.1-§5.4 and binds every file and validator, so it needs a numbered change.

**Done when.** The six parse fixtures fail as `INVALID_SYNTAX`, and the loader cases pass. `just test` is green, with every example and spec block still valid, and `pre-commit run --all-files` leaves the fixtures' bytes unchanged.

### X33. Git is read one way: repository, state, gitlinks and symlinks for validator rules

- **Resolves:** F014, F026, F027, F088, F103 · **Size:** M · **Depends on:** X35, X04, X46 · **Decision:** decided

*Updated by X46 (2026-11-04): decision 4(a) becomes "a symlink is accepted only where the validator recognises it as a stand-in", and the `MISSING_INPUT_FILE` row reads "An input's `path` names a file (not a symlink, directory or gitlink), or something the validator recognises as standing in for one (§5)". X33 no longer depends on X46's list, which is gone.*

**The problem.** The specification lets a validator read "git" without saying which repository or which state it reads. A symlink, a path under `.git/` or a path through a submodule therefore gets different verdicts from different git calls (F014). COMMIT_NOT_FOUND drops RFC 0007:195's "the declaring analysis's repository", and nothing says what a path read at a commit does at a gitlink (F026). INPUT_NOT_TRACKED names no repository (F027), and an input is "committed" in the prose but "tracked" in the rule (F103). COMMIT_NOT_ANCESTOR compares with "the commit the declaration is in", which a working-tree file doesn't have (F088).

**The fix.**
- Tidy-up RFC: a new change, "Git is read one way", appended after change 29 under the next free number. It is cited by title until the plan's new changes (X37, X34, X43, X40, X42, X49, X46) are numbered together. It records the five choices below. Design §11 lists it.
- Spec §3, after X35's sentences, a paragraph **Reading git**. A path is joined to the declaring folder, and its `..` is removed as text. In the working tree, it names an entry of the work tree. The work tree is every entry on disk below the repository's top level, tracked, untracked or ignored, except `.git/` and everything below it. A symlink is an entry and is never followed. A checked-out submodule's entries are included and read in the submodule's own repository. At a commit, a path names an entry of that commit's tree. When it finds a file or an analysis, a gitlink continues into the submodule at its pinned commit. A gitlink is never crossed when deciding whether an input is tracked. COMMIT_NOT_FOUND, COMMIT_NOT_ANCESTOR, INPUT_NOT_TRACKED and resolving a path at a commit all read the innermost repository that holds the file: for a file in a checked-out submodule, that is the submodule's own repository. X04's "root's repository, its submodules included" bounds only DECLARATION_OUTSIDE_REPOSITORY. §2's validator bullet points here.
- §5 Inputs and `Input`'s description: "tracked in git" replaces "committed".
- §9 rows, final text (X38 and X46 point here):
  - MISSING_INPUT_FILE: "An input's `path` names a file (not a symlink, directory or gitlink) or one of §5's stand-ins."
  - INPUT_NOT_TRACKED: "An input whose file or stand-in exists has it in the index of the repository that holds the input's analysis or, at a commit, in that commit's tree there."
  - COMMIT_NOT_FOUND: "…names a commit the declaring analysis's repository has" (RFC 0007:195).

  - MISSING_DECLARED_ANALYSIS: the code for a symlink on a declaration's path, chosen in (5).

**Decision** (Tom, 2026-11-04: decided as recommended).

(b) Committed at HEAD. Either way, an input in a plain data submodule exists but fails INPUT_NOT_TRACKED, in the working tree and at a commit alike, since RFC 0006:24 and :65 send another repository's data through a recipe. An untracked or ignored input fails the same way.

(b) Followed only for paths read from an analysis at a commit, which RFC 0004:63 and RFC 0007:183 and :200 require. (a, recommended: F014's suggestion and git's own reading) It isn't followed, and the declaration fails MISSING_DECLARED_ANALYSIS. (b) It is followed when its target stays inside the repository, and identity uses the resolved path. (c) A new code.

**Why this route.** No RFC, design section or tidy-up change says which repository or git state a validator reads (F027, F103), how a gitlink is crossed (F026), which commit stands for the working tree (F088), or whether a symlink is followed (F014). ### X37. Records are strict JSON documents; a broken record is `missing`, reported as INVALID_RECORD

- **Resolves:** F020, F050, F100 · **Size:** M · **Depends on:** X31, X03, X09 · **Decision:** decided

**The problem.** RFC 0005 judges a record only in the check layer: a file that breaks a rule of its §1 or §2 leaves its result `missing` (0005:142, :199). The specification also puts records in the parse and schema layers as documents, and nothing records that departure. It never names the parser a record is read with: §2 says a YAML 1.2 loader, and §3 and §9 say well-formed JSON. Stock JSON parsers keep the last of two repeated names, and the harness reads records only as YAML (F020). Nothing says which code a checker reports when a record breaks a schema rule (F050), and the `missing` row leaves out a record that breaks a parse rule (F100).

**The fix.**
- Tidy-up RFC, a new change 30 under Tightening, "A record is strict JSON, judged as a document". A record is RFC 8259 JSON text in UTF-8 with no byte order mark. It uses only JSON's forms: no comment, trailing comma, `NaN` or `Infinity` (`INVALID_SYNTAX`). A repeated name in an object is `DUPLICATE_KEY`. Its strings and numbers follow change 29's "Every document" paragraph from X31. A record that breaks a parse or schema rule leaves its result `missing`, reported as `INVALID_RECORD`. Add it to the migration list and to design §11.
- Spec §2: the parse row reads "a strict YAML 1.2 loader (`astra.yaml`) or a strict JSON parser (records)". The checker bullet says that a record which breaks a parse or schema rule leaves its result `missing`.
- Spec §3 Records: state the rule above. Add that `json.loads` and `JSON.parse` keep the last repeated name, so a checker needs a parser that reports repeated names.
- §9, one wording shared with X31. `INVALID_SYNTAX` reads: "Every `astra.yaml` is well-formed YAML 1.2 under the core schema (with no `%YAML` directive other than 1.2), every record is RFC 8259 JSON text in UTF-8 with no byte order mark, and in every document each string is a sequence of Unicode scalar values and each number is finite and within double range." `DUPLICATE_KEY` adds "and every name of a record's object". Build `INVALID_RECORD` on X03's wording: "The record at each planned result's place breaks no parse rule, and no schema rule against …". The `missing` status row adds "(including a record that breaks a parse or schema rule)". The check introduction and §10 say that a checker reports `INVALID_RECORD`, with each underlying code as its reason: the parser's code, or the one `rules.json` gives the error. Regenerate `rules.json`.

- Fixtures: convert the 16 record fixtures to `.json`. Each invalid one keeps its two header lines in a sidecar `<stem>.header`. **Decision** (Tom, 2026-11-04: decided as recommended). ### X38. No cascades: a rule isn't checked where another broken rule leaves its subject undetermined

- **Resolves:** F052, F091, F107 · **Size:** M · **Depends on:** X34 · **Decision:** decided

**The problem.** A validator "reports every parse, schema and validator rule the analysis breaks" (spec §2). The catalogue never says whether a rule counts as broken when another broken rule leaves its subject undetermined, so for one fault a tool can report one code or a cascade of them. A missing or malformed `spec_version` can also break `UNSUPPORTED_SPEC_VERSION`, and then there is no schema to read the file by (F052). An unreadable commit or remote also breaks `MISSING_DECLARED_ANALYSIS`, and every address through it breaks `UNRESOLVED_ADDRESS` (F107). A universe whose `derived_from` doesn't resolve, or whose chain loops, has no effective selection. As worded, `from_loop` escapes `DERIVED_FROM_CYCLE`, and "every analysis it drives" reads two ways (F091).

**The fix.**
- In §9's preface, add a paragraph **What is checked** after the layer table. It is not a subsection, so the extractor is unaffected. "A validator reports every rule broken, but not one that follows from another. A rule is checked only where what it is about is determined. Where another broken rule leaves that undetermined, the rule isn't checked and isn't reported. Two rules whose subjects are both determined are both reported, even when one edit fixes both."
  - A file that breaks a parse or schema rule isn't checked by the validator layer. Neither is a file whose well-formed `spec_version` the validator has no schema for (`UNSUPPORTED_SPEC_VERSION`).
  - A declared analysis is undetermined when its own file can't be read: its declaration breaks `DECLARATION_OUTSIDE_REPOSITORY`, `COMMIT_NOT_FOUND`, `REMOTE_UNREACHABLE` or `MISSING_DECLARED_ANALYSIS`, or its file is one of the files above. Nothing it holds is then checked: not `MISSING_DECISION` or `PARTIALLY_DRIVEN` over its decisions, and not any address through it.
  - With X45: after a `REMOTE_UNREACHABLE` on an outer declaration, `DECLARED_ANALYSIS_INVALID` on that declaration isn't checked, nor is the unreached analysis with every address into it. The outer analysis it read stays determined, so its `MISSING_DECLARED_ANALYSIS` and addresses into it (`upstream.summary#u1`) are still checked.
  - An address that doesn't resolve leaves its element undetermined. An input whose path names no file, and nothing the tooling recognises as standing in for one (`MISSING_INPUT_FILE`, X46), leaves `INPUT_NOT_TRACKED` unchecked.
  - A universe has no effective selection when its `derived_from` doesn't resolve, or when its chain loops or reaches a universe that has none. For that universe, `MISSING_DECISION`, `INACTIVE_DECISION`, `MISSING_REQUIRED_OPTION`, `INCOMPATIBLE_OPTIONS`, `EXCLUDED_OPTION_SELECTED`, `CONFLICTING_SELECTIONS`, `NOT_BUILT_IN_UNIVERSE`, `UNDRIVEN_DECLARATION` and `INPUT_NOT_BUILT` aren't checked. `UNRESOLVED_ADDRESS` and `UNKNOWN_OPTION` are still checked on its own `decisions`. `PARTIALLY_DRIVEN` is judged over the driver's other universes, counted as X51 (A) counts them.
- §2 validator role: it "reports every parse, schema and validator rule the analysis breaks, except those §9's What is checked leaves unchecked".
- §1 Versions: "A validator implements one version of this specification, its own, and has that version's schema. It may have the schemas of other versions. A file whose `spec_version` is missing or malformed is validated against the schema of the validator's own version, and every schema error is reported." Since §1 and change 9 are edited here, X59's order sentence lands with them: "Versions are ordered by MAJOR, then MINOR, then PATCH, each compared as an integer." This fix needs no order itself, and F098 stays X59's. The versioning policy stays deferred, and design §12 gets a cross-reference.
- §7 Validity: a rule broken inside an analysis from a commit is reported twice. `DECLARED_ANALYSIS_INVALID` goes on the declaration, in the file the author can edit. The inner code goes at its place in the commit's file, which says why.
- Scope these rows:
  - `UNSUPPORTED_SPEC_VERSION`: "Every `astra.yaml` in the stack whose `spec_version` is well-formed states one the validator has the schema for."
  - `MISSING_DECLARED_ANALYSIS`: "…at a commit the repository or remote has".
  - `DECLARED_ANALYSIS_INVALID`, one text shared with X45: "An analysis from a commit whose `astra.yaml` can be read, in a version the validator has the schema for, is valid at that commit with everything it declares, `REMOTE_UNREACHABLE` aside."
  - `UNRESOLVED_ADDRESS`: only through declarations that can be read.

### X51. When RFC: driving counts only the universes where something reached through the chain is active

- **Resolves:** F002 · **Size:** M · **Depends on:** X34, X13 · **Decision:** decided

**The problem.** Driving means setting keys through a declaration. Take a driver universe in which `when` switches off every decision reached through a driven declaration. That universe can't set a key (INACTIVE_DECISION). If it sets none, PARTIALLY_DRIVEN fails, and if no universe sets one, UNDRIVEN_DECLARATION fails. So every way of writing such a stack is invalid (F002, whose evidence stack passes the schema layer). F002 also names design §13's point: an analysis with no decisions can never be "driven". That point is about composition, not `when`, and X34 settles it.

**The fix.**
- When RFC draft, a paragraph under modification 4, "Driving where decisions are switched off". A universe counts for a chain of declarations only if some decision reached through the chain (the declared analysis's, or one further along any chain through it) is active in its effective selection. The chain is driven by all of the universes that count, or by none. A universe that doesn't count takes that status, so a driven analysis is built there with nothing to set. If no universe counts, the chain is read as X34's no-decisions clause reads an analysis with no decisions: driven by every universe of the driver when its analysis has no universes of its own, and by none otherwise. Use F002's `cal`/`raw` stack as the example, and give `stage` a second output, `count`, with no `when`, which reads `root.table`. The root reads `stage.count` without a pin. Table: `cal` counts and drives `stage`. `raw` and `raw_derived` don't count, so they take "driven" and build `stage.count`. The stack is valid.
- §7 'Driving' (l.699-700) and the §9 `PARTIALLY_DRIVEN` row (l.1140) use X34's sentence plus the exemption: "A chain of declarations from the driver's file is driven by all of its universes or by none, counting only the universes in which a decision reached through the chain is active. Any other universe takes the chain's status." Then run `just gen-schema`.
- The no-decisions clause (option iii: an analysis with no decisions and no universes of its own is driven by every universe of its driver) is not part of this fix: it moves to X34's change 30, which defines "drives".

**Decided.** (A): a chain is judged only over the universes in which a decision reached through it is active, and the others take its status. Not (B), which forces a dummy decision to keep one active everywhere, nor (C), which lets a universe set a switched-off decision as a marker and weakens INACTIVE_DECISION.

**Why this route.** The skip is about decisions switched off by `when`. The draft's principle ("nothing can use, read or test an element where that element is switched off") and modification 4 ("the same within one analysis and across analyses") cover it, but they don't say what it means for driving. The tidy-up changes don't decide it.

**Done when.** The regenerated `rules.json` passes `just check-generated`. By hand: F002's stack with `stage.count` is valid. The u1/u2 `stage.inner` stack fails PARTIALLY_DRIVEN (u2 drives `stage` through `stage.inner.d`, u1 counts and doesn't drive it).

### X54. Check phase: define a commit's files and the bytes a hash covers, pinned to one byte string, with an `unknown` status

- **Resolves:** F044, F071, F073 · **Size:** M · **Depends on:** X08, X33, X46, X53 · **Decision:** decided

*Updated by X46 (2026-11-04): "a stand-in on X46's list" becomes "a stand-in the checker recognises". Where it recognises none, the input is `unknown`; two checkers may then differ, which Tom accepted.*

**The problem.** The specification relies on "a commit's files" and on "the content" of an input or result, and defines neither. It doesn't say which paths a driven recipe gets, or which bytes count: git's blob, or a checkout under `.gitattributes`, `core.eol` and the reader's local config. So `NOT_AT_COMMIT` has no single reading (F044). Under `eol`, `ident`, `working-tree-encoding` or a clone's `core.autocrlf=true`, two conforming checkers disagree on `present` and `unchanged` (F073). A checker without an input's bytes, behind a stand-in that carries no content hash, has no true status to give (F071).

**The fix.**
- Now: design §13 gets an open point, "**A commit's files and the bytes a hash covers**", with the questions and options below. Its answers are recommendations pending Tom, not settled. Nothing else changes in this phase.
- Check and runner phase, through the RFC that amends RFC 0005 §4 (and its status list) and RFC 0007 §5 and §7:
  - §8 defines, for input and result files only (what `content_hash` and `size` cover, and what recipes read), and for what a driven recipe gets on disk: a commit's files are its tree, with submodules as X33's answer to its (3) reaches them. Their content is the bytes git checks out under the commit's own `.gitattributes` with `core.autocrlf=false` and `core.eol=lf`, so one byte string on every machine. A stand-in on X46's list is resolved to the content it names. A working-tree file's content is that of the blob git would stage from it. Recipes get those bytes, or the runner refuses. `NOT_AT_COMMIT`, §7 'Driving' and 'Results', `present`, `STALE_RESULT` and `STALE_INPUT` cite the definition. Which bytes a validator parses for an `astra.yaml` at a commit is current-phase work, with X32 or X33.
  - provenance.yaml: X08 rewrites the `content_hash` and `size` descriptions; this fix only adds the reference to the definition, and to it in `Generation.commit`.
  - A duty: the `.gitattributes` git applies to an analysis's inputs and `results/` SHOULD set no text or eol conversion (`-text`), no `ident`, no `working-tree-encoding`, and no filter except a stand-in's. Then blob, checkout and working tree agree.
  - §8 'When a result is unchanged': an input's content is unchanged when its bytes have the record's hash and size; or when the blob or stand-in git holds for it (at the path, or where X46's list puts the stand-in, such as `<path>.dvc`) and the attributes git applies to the path are the same at the record's commit and now (X53's frame); or when a stand-in names the record's SHA-256 and size. A stand-in naming another hash or size is a change. Otherwise the result is `unknown`, never `unchanged`. A result that reads an `unknown` result is `unknown` unless something else makes it stale; X53's last item is amended to match. `STALE_INPUT` refuses to build from an `unknown` result, and `STALE_EVIDENCE` passes the status on.
  - §9: the check intro ("A result for which every rule holds is `unchanged`"), the record-status lists in §8 'Statuses' and §9 ("`unchanged`, `stale` or `missing`"), the `STALE_RESULT` row's status cell, and a new `unknown` row in the Statuses table.

**Decision** (Tom, 2026-11-04: decided as recommended).
1. *Which files.* (a, recommended) The tree with submodules: `Generation.commit` "pins every file of the code", and iris_pipeline's `..` and a recipe's `../src` work. (b) The analysis's directory only.
2. *Which bytes at a commit.* (a, recommended) The pinned checkout above: what recipes read. (b) The raw blob: no checkout needed, but under `ident` it differs from what the recipe read. (c) Left to tooling.
3. *Which bytes in the working tree.* (a, recommended) The definition applied to the blob git would stage, and the runner gives recipes those bytes or refuses: one hash on every machine. (b) The bytes as they are, with the SHOULD as the only remedy: an `autocrlf=true` clone records other hashes.
4. *Who reports a breach of the SHOULD.* (a, recommended) A runner warning: the definition already keeps hashes machine-independent. (b) A check-layer row. (c) A validator SHOULD reading `.gitattributes`: current-phase work, routed tidy-up RFC.
5. *Without the bytes.* (a, recommended) `unknown`. (b) The checker MUST fetch them, or fails. (c) `stale`, which contradicts its definition.

**Why this route.** RFC 0005 §4 leaves the method open ("How a checker finds this out is up to the tooling"), and RFC 0007 §7 says only "exactly those files". Scoped to input and result content and recipes, every answer binds checkers and runners alone, and design §13 defers such points to the check and runner phase.

**Done when.** Now: design §13 lists the five questions. Later: check fixtures give one status for an `eol=crlf`, `ident` or `autocrlf=true` input whichever bytes a tool reads, `stale` when a `.gitattributes` change alone alters the checkout (same blob), `unknown` for a WORM key without bytes, and a refused build from an `unknown` result.

### X56. Runner phase: define a change for UNCOMMITTED_CHANGES (row exemption restored now)

- **Resolves:** F045 · **Partly:** F053 · **Size:** S · **Depends on:** X40, X02 · **Decision:** decided

**The problem.** `UNCOMMITTED_CHANGES` (RFC 0005 §3, spec §8 and the §9 row) refuses a run "while the repository has changes", but nothing defines a change. Untracked files, ignored files, submodules, earlier runs' records and results, and tools' caches are all left open, so a runner using `git diff HEAD` and one using `git status` disagree on the same checkout (F045). Separately, the §9 row drops the exemption RFC 0007 §2 and §7 grant to what a tool keeps of, or puts in place to drive, an analysis from a commit (F045). Whether a hand edit under `results/` is a change is already settled by RFC 0005 §3's "itself", which X40 restores (F053).

**The fix.**
- Now, just do, folded into X40's row edit and agreed with X02's §7 "Driving" sentence: the `UNCOMMITTED_CHANGES` row reads "A runner runs only when the repository has no changes other than the results and records it writes itself, and what a tool keeps of, or puts in place to drive, an analysis from a commit", Source `0005 §3, 0007 §2, 0007 §7`. Regenerate `rules.json`.
- Now: an open point in design §13 giving options (a)–(e) below and the recommendation.
- Runner phase, together: amend RFC 0005 §3 to define a change; write it in §8 "What a runner does" and the §9 row; add after `UNDECLARED_READ`: "An untracked or ignored file isn't pinned by the commit, so a recipe uses one only if it is part of the environment or made only from the files at the commit the run records (a bytecode cache, say)"; regenerate `rules.json`.
- The definition, whatever option: it holds whatever each machine's git configuration says. Submodules are checked whatever `submodule.<name>.ignore` or `diff.ignoreSubmodules` say, since a committed `.gitmodules` `ignore = all` would hide a submodule off its pinned commit. Reference form: `git status --porcelain=v2 --untracked-files=all --ignore-submodules=none`, read in each submodule too, with the exemptions applied there (a runner's record in a submodule analysis shows in the parent as ` M sub`). A tool's cache inside the repository is exempt only under an analysis's `.astra/` or in an ignored place.

**Decision** (Tom, 2026-11-04: decided as recommended). A change is what that `git status` reports, ignored files left out, plus one exemption rule:
(a) every analysis's `.astra/records/` and `results/`, whichever run wrote them. This amends RFC 0005 §3's "itself" and goes beyond defining a change: a hand edit there passes, and the runner-phase text replaces the wording X40 restores.
(b) tracked changes only (`git diff HEAD`): untracked helper code runs unpinned.
(c) ignored files count: any build cache refuses the run.
(d) only what this run writes: earlier uncommitted records force a commit between runs.
(e) every record, and any run's result whose bytes match its record's SHA-256: a hand edit is a change.
Recommended: (e). It keeps "itself" (a runner, not a hand), reverses nothing, and needs no commit between runs.
Sub-decision, which ignore rules count: (i) committed `.gitignore` files only (`git ls-files --others --exclude-per-directory=.gitignore`), the same on every machine (recommended); (ii) `.git/info/exclude` and `core.excludesFile` too, which vary by machine.

**Why this route.** The row edit restores RFC 0007 §2 ("none of it is a change … that stops a runner from running") and §7 ("aren't a change that stops a runner from running"): just do. Defining a change goes beyond RFC 0005 §3's words, and only runners read it; design §12–13 defer runner semantics.

**Done when.** Now: the row matches RFC 0005 §3 and 0007 §2/§7, `just check-generated` passes, and design §13 lists the point. Runner phase: RFC 0005 §3, §8 and the row agree, and conformance fixtures cover an untracked helper (refuse), an ignored `__pycache__/` (run), an earlier run's record (run), a hand-edited result (refuse under e), an untracked file in a submodule (refuse) and a record in a submodule analysis's `.astra/records/` (run).

### X57. Bytes status judged only against a valid record

- **Resolves:** F046 · **Size:** S · **Depends on:** X08 · **Decision:** decided

**The problem.** §8 'Statuses' defines the record status against "a valid record" and the bytes status against "its record's hash and size". It never says whether a file at the result's place that breaks `INVALID_RECORD` or `MISSING_RECORD_INPUTS` still counts as "its record" for the bytes. So a `missing` result has no settled bytes status (F046). One checker reports `missing`/`present`, another `missing`/`absent` and `ABSENT_RESULT` too.

**The fix.**
- Now: add an open point to design §13, "Bytes without a valid record", with the recommended answer below, for the check phase to settle.
- At the check phase, in the spec:
  - §8 'Statuses' (:1007-1010): "… and one for its bytes, judged only against a valid record where its address says: `present` when the bytes at its default place have that record's hash and size, `absent` otherwise. A result with no valid record has nothing to match, so its bytes are `absent`."
  - §9 `present` row: "The result has a valid record where its address says, and the bytes at its default place hash to that record's `content_hash` and have its `size`". `absent` row: "Anything else: no valid record, or anything other than the recorded bytes at the default place, nothing or a stand-in included".
  - §9 `ABSENT_RESULT`: "Every result with a valid record has its recorded bytes at its default place". Scope X08's `MODIFIED_RESULT` row the same way. Neither is reported for a `missing` result, since neither row's scope includes one.
  - §9 `MISSING_RECORD`, `INVALID_RECORD`, `MISPLACED_RECORD`, `MISSING_RECORD_INPUTS`: Status when broken becomes "`missing`; bytes `absent`", so the preface ("what the result gets when the rule doesn't hold", :1157) stays true. A sentence after the statuses table would say the same but not reach `rules.json`, so the cells are recommended.
  - Run `just gen-schema` and commit the regenerated `rules.json`.
- Under A, a result whose record breaks a parse rule is also `missing`/`absent`, once X37 lands.

**Decision** (Tom, 2026-11-04: decided as recommended). Option A (recommended): judge the bytes only against a valid record, as above. Every result with no valid record gets `missing`/`absent` and no bytes code. RFC 0005:199 leans towards A (it writes "no record, or a file breaking a rule", setting such a file apart from a record) but never says what its bytes status is; RFC 0007:130 ("Their content hash and size are checked against the record") leans the same way. Option B: judge the bytes against whatever file sits at the result's place, valid or not. A record that lacks only a new input would then report `present`. But B has to say which broken files still give a usable hash (unparsable ones, one naming another result), and it trusts one field of a file the check layer rejects.

**Why this route.** RFC 0005 leans but doesn't settle the point, so there is no source text to restore. Only checkers' reports depend on it: nothing in the schema or validator phase reads a bytes status. Design §13 already defers check-phase points like this one ("One result, two names").

**Done when.** Design §13 lists the point. At the check phase, a conformance fixture passes: a result whose record lacks an `inputs` item, with matching bytes, reports `missing`/`absent` and no `ABSENT_RESULT`. `rules.json` has the scoped `ABSENT_RESULT` and `MODIFIED_RESULT` statements and `status` "`missing`; bytes `absent`" for the four record codes.

### X58. Check layer: state the stand-in MAY as one sentence now; settle what a check-layer level means later

- **Resolves:** F097 · **Partly:** F018, F149 · **Size:** S · **Depends on:** X08, X12 · **Decision:** decided

*Updated by X49 (2026-11-04): the §1 bullet below drops "next to X12's `PIN_LIKE_COMMENT` recommendation".*

**The problem.** §9's consequence table says what a level means in every layer but check. Its check row says "Nothing fails" and never says what a broken `MUST` or `SHOULD` leads to, while §8 says only that "`absent` is not an error" (F097). The check layer's one permission, RFC 0005's "tooling that recognises stand-ins (MAY)", sits in the statuses table as "(`MAY`)" (:1173), which the extractor doesn't read and §1's list of codeless keyworded statements leaves out (F097; it is also the MAY half of F018). The fix has two halves: the MAY half is done now, with X08, and the levels half waits for the check phase.

**The fix.**
- MAY half, in the same pass as X08. Statuses table, `modified` row: "`MODIFIED_RESULT` doesn't hold", followed by X08's SHA-256/size test, with no keyword. X08's bullet for §8 Statuses (:1011-1014) and :1173 is amended to drop its "(MAY)" in both places.
- After the check rule table, one sentence: "Only tooling that recognises stand-ins can check `MODIFIED_RESULT`, so a checker MAY leave it out. A checker that leaves it out gives such a result `absent`." This is the only place the permission is stated. §8 Statuses links to it.
- §1 Rules paragraph: add this permission to the list of keyworded statements without a code, next to X12's `PIN_LIKE_COMMENT` recommendation.
- The check table gets no `MAY` row, as X12 says ("only the runner table has `MAY` rows").
- Levels half, in the check phase. The §9 consequence table's check row, and design §7's, say what a level means there (see Decision).

**Decision** (Tom, 2026-11-04: decided as recommended). This is about what a level means in the check layer. (A) A broken `MUST` is reported as an error and a broken `SHOULD` as a warning, and nothing fails. `stale`, `missing` and `modified` are then errors and `absent` is a warning, as RFC 0005's "`absent` (not an error …)" implies. **Why this route.** The MAY half is just do: it restores RFC 0005:46 and :87, and :246 supports the `absent` sentence. Without it, the current phase keeps a codeless `MAY` that §1 leaves out. **Done when.** No BCP 14 keyword is left in the statuses table, and a keyword scan outside rule rows finds only what §1 lists. `rules.json` has only X08's changes. Later, the check row states what both levels mean.

### X59. Versions: a checker's own version, and records of a version it has no schema for

- **Resolves:** F098 · **Size:** M · **Depends on:** X38, X30, X03, X37, X24 · **Decision:** decided

**The problem.** `INVALID_RECORD` picks a record's schema by comparing versions ("newer than the checker's"). But the specification defines no order on versions and gives a checker no version (F098). It also says nothing about a record of a smaller version that the checker has no schema for. For `astra.yaml`, `UNSUPPORTED_SPEC_VERSION` covers that case. RFC 0005 §2 and tidy-up change 22 say only "a later version", and a string comparison puts `0.10.0` before `0.9.0`. astra-spec ships only its own version's schemas, so today a checker can't read a record of an earlier release.

**The fix.**
- The order goes in this phase, with this fix. X38's §1 sentence reads a missing or malformed `spec_version` by the validator's own schema and needs no order, so only `INVALID_RECORD`'s "greater" does. §1 Versions gains "Versions are ordered by MAJOR, then MINOR, then PATCH, each compared as an integer." The RFC that adopts the code (NNNN, below) carries the same sentence, and so settles change 22's "later".
- §2 checker role: "A checker's version is the greatest `spec_version`, in §1's order, whose record schema it has. It has the closed record schema of every release of this specification from 0.1.0 up to its own." X30's versioned `$id`s (decided, option A) let these schemas sit side by side in one registry. Under today's shared `$id`, Ajv throws on the second schema, and `referencing` keeps only one (F151).
- Why from 0.1.0: records begin at 0.1.0 (RFC 0005, tidy-up change 10), and no release before it has a record schema. Moving 0.0.x files to 0.1 is migration under the versioning policy, not something a 0.1 checker reads. A pre-release (X30) is not a release here: its files state `0.1.0`, because `spec_version` has no pre-release form, so its records are read by 0.1.0's schema.
- astra-spec ships the schemas a checker must have. `just release X.Y.Z` copies the release's `provenance.schema.json` and `rules.json` into `src/astra/schema/releases/X.Y.Z/` (the error map gives `INVALID_RECORD`'s underlying code, X37). Each later release keeps every earlier copy. These files are never regenerated or edited. A test checks each against `git show vX.Y.Z:src/astra/schema/generated/<file>`. CLAUDE.md and design §3 name the directory.
- §9 check table, new row: `UNSUPPORTED_RECORD_VERSION` | MUST | "Every record whose `spec_version` is well-formed states the checker's own version, a greater one, or a smaller one whose record schema the checker has" | `missing` | `NNNN, §1`. NNNN is the number of the RFC that adopts the code and the policy, so X24's Source check accepts the row as it stands. Add the code to the `missing` status row. With the duty above, the code fires only for a version that was never released, a 0.0.x one included.
- `INVALID_RECORD`, written on X03 and X37's wording: "The record at each planned result's place, unless its `spec_version` is a smaller one whose record schema the checker doesn't have, breaks no parse rule, and no schema rule against the closed record schema of its `spec_version` (the checker's own when that is missing or malformed), or the open variant when that version is greater than the checker's".
- X38's "What is checked" paragraph gains: "A record whose version the checker can't read isn't checked by `INVALID_RECORD`."
- §8 "What a record holds", the §10 open-variant paragraph, design D10 and the design's `INVALID_RECORD` row: "newer" becomes "greater". §8 and §10 each gain a clause for the smaller case that links the new code. Design §12's versioning-policy item records that the duty starts at 0.1.0. Regenerate `rules.json`.

**Decided.** (1a) A new code, `UNSUPPORTED_RECORD_VERSION`, MUST, status `missing`, so that "this checker can't read the record" stays apart from "the record is broken"; folding the case into `INVALID_RECORD` (b) or using the open variant (c) were not taken. (2i) A checker MUST have the record schema of every release, scoped to every release from 0.1.0 on, so that upgrading a checker never turns old results `missing`; keeping only the checker's MAJOR (ii) or none (iii) were not taken.

**Why this route.** Only checkers read records, and no validator rule does. Spec §1 ("which versions a schema accepts, is left to ASTRA's versioning policy") and design §12 defer the policy, and the code and the order land with it in the check phase. Nothing writes records yet, so nothing breaks before then.

**Done when.**
- §1 states the order, §2 defines a checker's version and its duty, and `just gen-schema` passes with the new row.
- `src/astra/schema/releases/` holds the 0.1.0 copies after the 0.1.0 release, and the test ties each copy to its tag.
- Check-phase conformance cases cover: a `0.10.0` record read by a `0.9.x` checker (open variant); a 0.1.0 record read by a later checker (0.1.0's closed schema); a `0.0.9` record (the new code, `missing`); and a record with no `spec_version` (`INVALID_RECORD` only).
